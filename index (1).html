Why this exists

This isn't a hackathon looking for a cause. It's a cause looking for hackers.

Our teachers, ashram teams, organizers, and volunteers run into the same friction points over and over: a manual process here, information that's hard to find there, a bottleneck nobody's had the bandwidth to fix. Real problems, quietly stacking up.

The AOLF Hackathon exists to close that gap two ways at once: build a standing volunteer tech team that keeps showing up after the event ends, and along the way, ship real solutions to problems sourced directly from our own community and program teams, not invented for the occasion.

Every challenge you'll work on traces back to an actual person's answer to one question: if you had a magic wand, what would you fix?

A team that outlasts the event

The primary goal isn't a demo. It's the volunteers who keep building together afterward.

Real problems, not prompts

Every challenge is sourced from Operations, Marketing, Data, Executive teams, and a community-wide survey.

The path to build day

How it works

Nothing about this hackathon starts cold. The problems, the teams, and the plan are all in place before hour one.

Community input

We ask the community for real problems

PTCs, full-time teachers, ashram & retreat center teams, organizers, and volunteers answer one prompt: "if you had a magic wand, what problem would you solve?" That runs alongside direct input from Operations, Marketing, Data, and Executive teams.

Curation

Responses become a short list of challenges

We categorize every response and shape them into a limited set of structured hackathon challenges, each with context and success criteria, sized to roughly 4–5 teams for simpler problems and 7–8 for the more complex ones.

Application

You apply with a challenge and a plan

Problem statements are shared with every applicant before the event. Pick one or more that speak to you, and submit a short proposed approach along with your application.

Build

36 hours, start to finish

Actual building happens only during the event itself: one continuous, sunrise-to-sunrise sprint with mentors on hand throughout.

What's next

The team keeps going

Demos and a closing circle wrap the event, along with an open door to join the ongoing AOLF volunteer tech team for anyone who wants to keep building.
